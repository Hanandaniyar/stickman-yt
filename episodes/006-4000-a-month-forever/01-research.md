# 01 — Research (Shorts): "This number pays you $4,000 a month. Forever."

## Ringkasan untuk pemilik (Bahasa Indonesia)
- Aturan 4% **bukan "forever"**. Klaim aslinya: tarik 4% di tahun pertama, lalu naikkan sesuai inflasi; di data historis AS, uang bertahan **minimal 30 tahun** (Bengen: paling pendek ~33 tahun untuk portofolio 50/50; banyak kasus 50+ tahun). Kata "Forever" di hook harus dipatahkan di dalam video ("Not forever. About 30 years. And history says that's usually enough.").
- Angka terbaru: Morningstar (3 Des 2025) **3,9%** aman untuk 30 tahun (peluang 90%). Bengen sendiri (buku 2025) menaikkan angka terburuk historisnya ke **4,7%**.
- Hitungan: $1,2 juta × 4% = $48.000/tahun = **$4.000/bulan** (sebelum pajak). Dengan asumsi 7%/tahun: **~$984/bulan selama 30 tahun** atau **~$457/bulan selama 40 tahun**.
- S&P 500 1928–2025: ~**10,0%/tahun** nominal, ~**6,8%/tahun** setelah inflasi.
- Catatan penting untuk naskah: $4.000 di 30–40 tahun lagi nilainya jauh lebih kecil (inflasi). Kalau 7% dianggap return *riil*, angka sudah "dalam dolar hari ini". Pilih satu dan katakan jelas.

## 1. Origin of the 4% rule — what it actually claims

### William Bengen, "Determining Withdrawal Rates Using Historical Data," Journal of Financial Planning, October 1994
Source (FPA reprint, March 2004): https://www.financialplanningassociation.org/sites/default/files/2021-04/MAR04%20Determining%20Withdrawal%20Rates%20Using%20Historical%20Data.pdf
- Data: Ibbotson *Stocks, Bonds, Bills and Inflation: 1992 Yearbook*; retirements starting each year 1926–1976.
- Portfolio: 50% large-company stocks / 50% intermediate-term Treasuries, rebalanced. Recommends 50–75% stocks.
- Method: withdraw X% of the **starting** value in year 1, then raise that dollar amount by inflation every year.
- Key finding (quote): "Assuming a minimum requirement of 30 years of portfolio longevity, a first-year withdrawal of 4 percent ... should be safe. In no past case has it caused a portfolio to be exhausted before 33 years, and in most cases it will lead to portfolio lives of 50 years or longer."
- 4.25% "could exhaust a portfolio in as little as 28 years." 3% was "absolutely safe" (50+ years in every case). 5% is "risky"; 6%+ is "gambling."
- Assumes tax-deferred accounts (no capital-gains tax modeled). Charts were capped at 50 years.

### Trinity Study — Cooley, Hubbard & Walz (Trinity University), AAII Journal, Feb 1998
The 1998 AAII article is paywalled. I verified the numbers from the same authors' 1999 follow-up, which says its results are "equivalent to those reported in ... Cooley, et al., (1998)" for 3–7% withdrawals:
"Sustainable Withdrawal Rates From Your Retirement Portfolio," *Financial Counseling and Planning* 10(1), 1999. https://everywhereonce.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/trinity-study1.pdf
- Data: S&P 500 and long-term high-grade corporate bonds, 1926–1997, 15/20/25/30-year payout windows.
- "Success" = money not gone by the end of the period.
- 4% inflation-adjusted withdrawals over **30 years** (Table 2), share of historical periods that succeeded:
  - 100% stocks: 98%
  - 75% stocks / 25% bonds: 100%
  - 50% / 50%: 95%
  - 25% / 75%: 74%
- Abstract: "portfolios of at least 75% stock provide 4% to 5% inflation-adjusted withdrawals."

### "Forever" or 30 years? (honest line)
Both studies tested **30 years**, not forever. Use something like:
> "It's not forever. It's built to last about 30 years. In past U.S. markets, a 4% start lasted at least that long almost every time, and often left money over."

Don't say "guaranteed" or "it never fails." Trinity's 50/50 result is 95%, not 100%.

## 2. Recent views (2025–2026)

### Morningstar, "The State of Retirement Income: 2025" (Dec. 3, 2025; data as of Sept. 30, 2025)
https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/bltb73b87c5d0c70ead/The_State_of_Retirement_Income_2025.pdf
- **3.9%** = highest safe starting rate for fixed, inflation-adjusted spending. Assumes a 30-year horizon, a 90% chance of money left, and 30–50% stocks.
- Previous base cases: 3.3% (2021), 3.8% (2022), 4.0% (2023), 3.7% (2024).
- 40-year horizon: **3.3%**. 35-year horizon: 3.5%.
- Flexible spending methods allow starting rates up to **5.7%**.
- With historical (not forward-looking) returns, the safe rate is 4.4% for 50/50 and 4.7% for 90% stocks.
- The report says its rates do **not** include taxes or fees. Example given: 1% fees cut the 60/40 rate to 3.4%, and withdrawals from traditional IRAs and 401(k)s are taxed as ordinary income.
- Note: the next Morningstar report (for 2026–2027) usually comes out in early December. It wasn't out as of 2026-11-17, so recheck before upload.

### Bengen's new number: 4.7%
Book: *A Richer Retirement: Supercharging the 4% Rule to Spend More and Enjoy More* (Wiley, August 2025).
- U.S. News, republished by WTOP, July 28, 2026: Bengen raised the 30-year baseline to **4.7%**. Assumptions: seven asset classes (55% stocks / 40% bonds / 5% cash), every historical 30-year period since 1926, and a tax-deferred account. https://wtop.com/news/2026/07/the-4-rule-no-longer-works-for-retirees-says-the-man-who-invented-it/
- White Coat Investor, Sept. 21, 2025: Bengen calls 4.7% "a worst-case scenario." https://www.whitecoatinvestor.com/4-percent-rule-update-bill-bengen/
- Jonathan Clements (HumbleDollar), Aug. 17, 2025: Bengen says the rate "should be raised to at least 4.7%." https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/26004948-is-4-7-the-new-4-0-safe-withdrawal-rate?tab=book
- Safe line: "Even the man who invented the rule now says 4.7%, while other researchers say closer to 3.9%. Four percent sits right in the middle."

## 3. Compound math: how to reach $1.2 million

Target: $1,200,000 × 4% = $48,000/yr ÷ 12 = **$4,000/month** (first year, before tax).

Formula (future value of regular deposits):
`FV = PMT × ((1 + i)^n − 1) / i`, so `PMT = FV × i / ((1 + i)^n − 1)`

Assumptions: 7% average yearly return, deposit at the end of each month, monthly compounding (i = 0.07/12), no taxes, no fees, no market crashes in the math. Checked in Python by running the 360 months forward; it lands on exactly $1,200,000.

| Years | Monthly deposit (i = 7%/12) | Total you put in | Growth from compounding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | **$983.63** | $354,107 | $845,893 |
| 40 | **$457.18** | $219,444 | $980,556 |

Sensitivity: with 7% as an *effective* yearly rate (i = 1.07^(1/12) − 1), the deposits are $1,026 (30 yrs) and $486 (40 yrs). With one deposit at the end of each year: $12,704/yr (30 yrs) or $6,011/yr (40 yrs).

Script line: "About $1,000 a month for 30 years, or about $460 a month for 40, assuming a 7% average return."

Inflation caveat (my own calculation): if 7% is a *nominal* return, then $4,000 a month 30 years from now buys about what **$1,650** buys today at 3% inflation (about $1,230 after 40 years). If 7% is treated as a *real* (after-inflation) return, all figures are in today's dollars. 7% is close to the historical real return of the S&P 500 (see section 4), but a mixed stock/bond portfolio earns less. **Recommendation:** in the script, call 7% "after inflation, roughly the stock market's long-run average" and say "it's not guaranteed."

## 4. Long-run S&P 500 returns

- NYU Stern (Aswath Damodaran), "Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds and Bills," S&P 500 with dividends reinvested: $100 at the start of 1928 grew to $1,157,598.95 by the end of 2025. That's a compound rate of **~10.0% per year, nominal** (my calculation from his table: (11,575.99)^(1/98) − 1 = 10.02%). https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html
- Inflation over the same span, from CPI-U (FRED CPIAUCNS, Dec 1927 = 17.3, Dec 2025 = 324.054): ~3.0%/yr. https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCNS
- Return **after inflation: ~6.8% per year** (1.1002 / 1.0304 − 1). This is my calculation from the two primary sources above.
- Safe line: "Since 1928, U.S. stocks have averaged about 10% a year, or roughly 7% after inflation, with some brutal years along the way."

## 5. Required warnings (plain English for narration and description)
- "Markets can drop, sometimes by a third or more in a year or two. A crash early in retirement is the biggest danger." Bengen: 1973–74 stocks −37.2%; Morningstar: nearly 70% of failed simulations had lost money by year 5.
- "That $4,000 is before taxes. Money from a traditional 401(k) or IRA is taxed as income."
- "Inflation shrinks what $4,000 buys over time."
- "The 4% rule is a rule of thumb from past data, not a promise. Your situation is different. This isn't financial advice."
- "It's designed to last about 30 years, not forever."
