## 1. Why Your New Car Loses Thousands the Moment You Drive Home
*(59 chars)*. Alternatives: "The $1,072-a-Month Machine in Your Driveway" (43) / "Where Your Car Money Really Goes (It's Not Gas)" (47)

**Angle.** Open on a mystery: a stickman buys a shiny new car, drives it 1 mile out of the lot, and a price tag above it drops by thousands. Nothing about the car changed, so where did the money go? We follow the clues. **Clue 1:** AAA's cost breakdown shows the biggest cost of a new car isn't gas but **depreciation** (about $4,422/year). **Clue 2:** financing. Longer loans shrink the monthly payment but stretch the interest. **The answer:** George Akerlof's Nobel-winning **"Market for Lemons"**. Once a car is "used," buyers can't tell a good one from a lemon, so every used car gets priced like it *might* be a lemon. The drop is the price of **missing information**. Close with a neutral, mechanical takeaway: total cost of ownership vs. sticker price. No brand picks and no "buy used, not new" advice.

**Why US viewers care.** Most American households own a car, and it's usually their second-biggest expense after housing. Payments of $1,000+ a month are now common, and the car-buying, insurance, and refinancing search terms are evergreen.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 5 | Auto loans, auto insurance, and car buying platforms are among the highest-paying ad categories. |
| Search interest | 5 | "how much does it cost to own a car," "car depreciation," and "car payment calculator" are evergreen. The AAA and Edmunds data are in the news this fall. |
| Stickman fit / visual | 5 | Price tag crashing at the gate, a leaky wallet, lemons vs. shiny cars, a calendar stretching to 84 months, a pie of costs. It's a concrete object and a clear story from start to finish. |
| Ad safety | 5 | Consumer education, nonpartisan, no brands. |

**Key facts**
1. AAA *Your Driving Costs* 2026 (released **Sep 15, 2026**): the average annual cost to own and operate a new vehicle is **$12,863** (about **$1,072/month**). **Depreciation is the single largest cost at about $4,422/year**, over one-third of the total. Assumptions: 5 years, 75,000 miles, gas at $4.152/gal. Methodology changed from 2025 ($11,577), so don't compare years directly.
   Sources: https://mwg.aaa.com/news/press-release/september-15-2026 · https://www.kbb.com/car-news/aaa-hybrids-come-out-on-top-in-2026-ownership-cost-study/ · https://www.carpro.com/blog/aaa-study-new-vehicle-ownership-is-up-to-12863-per-year
2. Kelley Blue Book (Cox Automotive), **October 2026**: the average new-vehicle transaction price hit a record **$51,109**, up 2.1% from October 2025. Compact cars averaged **$28,030**.
   Sources: https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/oct-2026-atp-report/ · https://www.motor1.com/news/815071/average-new-cars-prices-october-2026-record/
3. Edmunds Q3 2026 (October 2026): average new-car payment **$787/month**, average APR **7.0%**, record average amount financed **$44,664**, and average lifetime interest **$9,938**. A record **21.2%** of new-car buyers committed to **$1,000+/month** payments.
   Sources: https://www.edmunds.com/press/new-car-financing-records-pile-up-in-q3-as-buyers-borrow-more-and-stretch-loans-longer-according-to-edmunds.html · https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2026/10/19/new-car-payments-hit-record-787-a-month-as-buyers-take-on-longer-loans-report-finds/
4. Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences **2001**: Akerlof, Spence, and Stiglitz, "for their analyses of markets with asymmetric information." Akerlof's 1970 paper used **used cars ("lemons")** as the example. Sellers know the car's quality and buyers don't, which pushes prices down.
   Source: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2001/press-release/
5. Derived (our arithmetic): $44,664 at 7.0% costs about **$884/mo over 60 months** (≈ $8,400 interest), **$761/mo over 72 months** (≈ $10,160), or **$674/mo over 84 months** (≈ $11,960). A smaller payment means more total interest. That's a ready-made "shrinking payment, growing pile" visual.

## 2. Why the Minimum Payment Keeps You in Credit Card Debt
*(55 chars)*. Alternatives: "How a $5,000 Credit Card Balance Takes 12 Years to Pay Off" (58) / "Compound Interest in Reverse: The Credit Card Trap" (49)

**Angle.** Episode 001 was money shrinking while it sits still. This is **compound interest running backwards**. The mystery: "You've paid $100 every month for a year. Why does the balance barely move?" Visual: a stickman shovels sand out of a hole while a hose (interest) pours it back in. Then he doubles his shovel ($200), and the hole empties in under 3 years. We explain APR → monthly rate, why the minimum payment formula is designed to be small, and why the payment size matters more than anything else. Mechanism only: no card brands, no debt-relief pitches, no shaming.

**Why US viewers care.** Holiday spending starts now, card balances are at a record, and most adults have carried a balance at some point.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 5 | Credit, consolidation, and banking advertisers pay top rates. |
| Search interest | 5 | "how to pay off credit card debt" and "credit card interest calculator" are evergreen, with a seasonal spike in Dec–Jan. |
| Stickman fit / visual | 5 | Hole and hose, treadmill, hourglass, two race tracks ($100 vs. $200). |
| Ad safety | 4 | Safe, but it may attract low-quality debt-relief ads. Keep the tone neutral. |

**Key facts**
1. NY Fed Household Debt and Credit Report (**Nov 10, 2026**): total household debt is **$19.1 trillion** (Q3 2026). Credit card balances rose $22B to **$1.29 trillion**, and **5.0%** of debt was in some stage of delinquency.
   Source: https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110
2. Federal Reserve G.19 (**Nov 6, 2026**, Q3 2026): average APR is **21.19%** on all accounts and **22.36%** on accounts assessed interest.
   Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm
3. Atlanta Fed payment diary data (via Pew, **Sep 30, 2026**): credit cards were the **most-used payment method in 2025 at 34%** of payments, up from 18% in 2015.
   Source: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/30/from-cash-to-credit-how-americans-payment-methods-have-changed-in-the-past-decade/
4. Derived (our arithmetic, recomputed today): $5,000 at 22.36% APR accrues about **$93** of interest in month one. Paying **$100/month** takes about **146 months (~12 years)** and about **$9,500 in interest**. Paying **$200/month** takes about **34 months** and about **$1,800 in interest**.

## 3. Who Really Gets Paid When You Tap Your Card?
*(45 chars)*. Alternatives: "What Happens in the 2 Seconds After You Tap Your Card" (52) / "The Hidden Fee Inside Every Cup of Coffee" (41)

**Angle.** A detective story in two seconds. A stickman taps a card for a $5 coffee, and we freeze time to follow the message: terminal → processor → card network → the bank that issued the card → "approved" → back to the café. That's the tech half (a message bouncing across data centers faster than a blink). Then the money half: the café doesn't receive the full $5. A slice is split among the issuing bank (interchange), the network, and the processor. **The twist:** merchants build that cost into everyday prices, so even people who pay cash help fund card rewards. Explain the mechanism neutrally. **Do not** take sides on pending legislation or the ongoing court case over the debit fee cap.

**Why US viewers care.** Almost every purchase goes through this system. It explains rewards cards, "cash discount" signs, surcharges at restaurants, and why some small shops set card minimums.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 5 | Credit cards, payments, and fintech are top-tier advertisers. |
| Search interest | 3 | "credit card processing fees" and "how do credit card rewards work" are steady. The full explainer angle is a curiosity search, not a big-volume keyword. |
| Stickman fit / visual | 5 | Frozen-time journey, the $5 coffee sliced like a pie, 900 million taps a day as a flood of tiny cards. |
| Ad safety | 4 | Safe if we stay mechanical. Swipe-fee politics (legislation, lawsuits) must be avoided. |

**Key facts**
1. Nilson Report: US merchants paid **$198.25 billion** in card processing fees in **2025** (2024: $187.20 billion, up 8.7%), at a weighted average of **1.59%** of purchase volume. US card purchase volume was **$12.498 trillion** in 2025 (Issue 1303, March 2026).
   Sources: https://nilsonreport.com/merchant-processing-fees-industry-investment/ · https://nilsonreport.com/articles/merchant-processing-fees-in-the-united-states-2025/
2. Visa Fiscal 2025 Annual Report: **257.5 billion** transactions processed on Visa's network (up from 233.8B), and about **329 billion** Visa-branded transactions overall, or roughly **901 million per day**.
   Source: https://s29.q4cdn.com/385744025/files/doc_downloads/2025/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf
3. Federal Reserve Regulation II data: the Fed publishes average debit interchange fees per transaction by network (last updated Dec 2025, covering 2023). Covered-issuer debit fees are capped by rule (21¢ + 0.05% + a 1¢ fraud adjustment). An Oct 2026 appeals court ruling has put that cap in legal limbo, so **don't script the cap as settled**. Mention debit vs. credit only in general terms.
   Sources: https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/regii-average-interchange-fee.htm · https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/10/federal-appeals-court-strikes-down-the-feds-debit-card-fee-cap/
4. Atlanta Fed payment diary (via Pew, Sep 30, 2026): cash fell from **33% of payments (2015) to 14% (2025)**, and credit cards rose from 18% to **34%**.
   Source: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/30/from-cash-to-credit-how-americans-payment-methods-have-changed-in-the-past-decade/
5. Derived (our arithmetic): at the 1.59% average, a $5 coffee carries about **8¢** in processing cost. $198.25B ÷ $12.498T ≈ 1.59%, which is consistent with Nilson.

## 4. Why Your Home Insurance Bill Keeps Going Up
*(46 chars)*. Alternatives: "I Never Filed a Claim. Why Did My Home Insurance Go Up?" (55) / "The Science Behind Your Rising Home Insurance" (45)

**Angle.** The mystery: a stickman's house has never had a claim, yet the premium jumps every year. Clues: (1) **insurance is a shared pool**. Everyone's premiums fill one tank, and storms elsewhere drain it. (2) **Replacement cost**. Lumber, labor, and roofing cost more, so rebuilding the same house costs more. (3) **The physics of risk**: hail energy, wind load, wildfire spread, and why insurers price by ZIP code. (4) **Insurers buy insurance too** (reinsurance). Keep it neutral: present climate-loss data as insurer and Treasury data, without blame and without policy opinions.

**Why US viewers care.** Mortgage lenders require homeowners insurance, so a higher premium raises the monthly housing payment through escrow. It affects home affordability, especially in Florida, Texas, California, and the Gulf states.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 5 | Insurance is one of the most expensive ad categories on YouTube. |
| Search interest | 4 | "why is my home insurance going up" spikes at renewal time and after disasters. It's strongest regionally. |
| Stickman fit / visual | 4 | House, storm, hail, bricks with rising price tags, a shared water tank draining. Fun, but slightly more explanation-heavy. |
| Ad safety | 3 | Climate framing can read as political, and disasters are sensitive. Requires careful, neutral wording and no disaster footage-style imagery. |

**Key facts**
1. US Treasury Federal Insurance Office (report released **Jan 16, 2025**, data for 2018–2022, 246M+ policies, about 80% of the market): average homeowners premiums per policy rose **8.7% faster than inflation** from 2018 to 2022. Policyholders in the **riskiest 20% of ZIP codes** paid **$2,321** on average, **82% more** than in the lowest-risk 20%.
   Source: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2791
2. Insurify (2026 report): the national average premium is projected to rise about **4% to ~$3,057** in 2026, after a **12% jump in 2025**, a fifth straight year of increases. This is a commercial estimate, so label it as such.
   Source: https://insurify.com/homeowners-insurance/insights/rates-increase-first-half-2026
3. Insurance.com (2026 data): **Florida** has the highest average premium at **$8,471** vs. a national average of **$2,872**. National averages differ by source ($2,400–$3,100) because of different policy assumptions, so show a range, not one number.
   Source: https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/property/floridians-pay-181-more-for-home-insurance-than-us-average--571385.aspx
4. Brookings (summary of the FIO data and the drivers, including construction-cost inflation).
   Source: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/homeowners-insurance-in-an-era-of-climate-change

## 5. Is AI Making Your Electric Bill Higher? The Physics of Power
*(61 chars)*. Alternatives: "Why AI Needs So Much Electricity (and Who Pays)" (46) / "The Hidden Cost of Every AI Prompt" (34)

**Angle.** Unchanged from the Nov 12 list, with numbers refreshed. Why chips turn electricity into heat, why data centers need cooling, and how the grid is a shared pipe whose **capacity** costs money. When demand outruns supply, capacity prices rise and grid costs are spread across all customers. Present both sides (gas prices, aging grid, and rate design also matter).

**Why US viewers care.** Electricity is rising faster than overall inflation in many regions, and AI is part of daily life.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 4 | Tech plus energy/home is solid, a bit below pure personal finance. |
| Search interest | 4 | Trending, but regional (PJM states, Texas, Virginia). |
| Stickman fit / visual | 4 | Hot chips, giant fans, a grid as water pipes, a crowded bridge for capacity. The mystery is weaker because the answer is "partly, depends where you live." |
| Ad safety | 4 | Contested and sometimes politicized locally. Stay evidence-based and neutral. |

**Key facts**
1. BLS CPI, September 2026 (released **Oct 14, 2026**): electricity rose **4.4%** over 12 months.
   Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm
2. BLS CPI, October 2026 (released **Nov 10, 2026**): electricity rose **0.9% in the month**. BLS **did not publish 12-month changes** for October because October 2025 data were not collected during the 2025 shutdown, so use the September y/y figure.
   Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_11102026.htm
3. DOE/LBNL (**Dec 20, 2024**): data centers used about **4.4%** of US electricity (176 TWh) in 2023, projected at **6.7%–12%** by 2028.
   Source: https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers
4. EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook (2026 editions): commercial electricity use (which includes data centers) is expected to **surpass residential for the first time on record in 2027**, and the fastest data-center demand growth is in the **ERCOT and PJM** regions. Check the November 10, 2026 STEO before scripting.
   Sources: https://www.utilitydive.com/news/data-centers-electricity-residential-rates-eia/820342/ · https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/
5. PJM 2028/29 capacity auction (**Jul 14, 2026**): cleared at the **$325/MW-day cap**, about 6.8 GW short of the reliability target. PJM cited data-center-driven demand growth.
   Source: https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-capacity-auction-price-cap-reserve-shortfall-grows/825282/
