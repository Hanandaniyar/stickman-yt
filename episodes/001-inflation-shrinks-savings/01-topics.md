## Ringkasan untuk pemilik (Bahasa Indonesia)

- **Topik pilihan pemilik:** kenapa inflasi bikin tabungan menyusut. Ini satu entri lengkap, bukan 5 usulan.
- **Judul kerja:** *Why Your Savings Shrink Even When the Balance Doesn't* (52 karakter). Ada 3 judul alternatif di bawah.
- **Sudut pandang:** "gerak relatif" ala fisika. Saldo tabungan diam, tapi harga bergerak seperti arus sungai. Stickman yang berdiri diam di eskalator turun tetap mundur. Kita tidak memberi saran investasi; kita hanya menjelaskan *suku bunga riil = bunga − inflasi*.
- **Angka terbaru (sudah dicek):** CPI AS naik **3,7%** dalam 12 bulan sampai September 2026 (BLS, 14 Okt 2026). Bunga tabungan rata-rata nasional cuma **0,38%** (FDIC, 19 Okt 2026). Artinya daya beli tabungan biasa turun sekitar **3,2% per tahun**. $100 di Sep 2020 hanya setara **sekitar $77** di Sep 2026.
- **Catatan penting untuk penulis naskah:** BLS **tidak menerbitkan** angka inflasi 12 bulan untuk Oktober 2026, karena data Oktober 2025 tidak dikumpulkan saat shutdown. Jadi pakai angka **September 2026 (3,7%)** sebagai angka tahunan terbaru.

## Topic entry

### Working title
**Why Your Savings Shrink Even When the Balance Doesn't** (52 chars)

**Alternative titles**

2. Your Bank Account Is Standing Still on a Down Escalator (55 chars)
3. The Hidden Leak in Your Savings Account, Explained (50 chars)

### Angle: what makes this explainer different
Most inflation videos list "prices go up" and stop there. This one uses a **physics framing: relative motion**.
- Your balance is a stickman standing still. Prices are a river current (or a down escalator) moving under him. Measured against the bank statement, he hasn't moved. Measured against what money can *buy*, he's drifting backward.
- That gives us one clean, visual idea: **real interest rate ≈ interest rate − inflation**. A stickman walking up the escalator at 0.38%/yr while it moves down at 3.7%/yr still loses ground. One walking faster only roughly keeps pace.
- We end with a neutral "Rule of 72" visual: at ~3.7% inflation, prices double in about 19–20 years, which turns an abstract percentage into a timeline.
- No product picks and no "buy X." We only explain the mechanism and the vocabulary (CPI, APY, nominal vs. real).

### Why US viewers care
- **Money:** Most Americans keep cash in savings accounts paying close to the national average (0.38%), which is far below current inflation. They lose purchasing power every year without seeing a smaller number on the statement.
- **Daily life:** Gasoline is up 34.2% year over year as of September 2026, so viewers feel inflation at the pump right now.
- **Decisions:** Understanding "real" returns helps people judge emergency funds, raises at work ("Is my 3% raise actually a pay cut?"), and the rates banks advertise.
- **Evergreen:** The mechanism never goes out of date. Only the numbers need a refresh, so the video keeps earning views long after release.

### Key facts (verified, with dates)
1. **Headline inflation is 3.7% a year.** "Over the last 12 months, the all items index increased 3.7 percent before seasonal adjustment" (CPI-U, 12 months ending **September 2026**; released Oct 14, 2026). Core CPI (excluding food and energy) rose 2.6%, and the energy index rose 20.4%, with **gasoline up 34.2%**. Food rose 2.5%.
   Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm
   *Caveat:* The October 2026 CPI (released Nov 10, 2026) does **not** include a 12-month change: "BLS cannot publish 12-month percent changes for October 2026 for indexes where October 2025 data are unavailable," because October 2025 data weren't collected during the lapse in appropriations. The monthly core change in October 2026 was +0.3%.
   Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm

2. **The typical savings account pays 0.38%.** FDIC national average savings deposit rate: **0.38%** (rate-cap table as of **Oct 19, 2026**).
   Source: https://www.fdic.gov/national-rates-and-rate-caps
   → Derived (our arithmetic): real return ≈ 1.0038 / 1.0374 − 1 ≈ **−3.2% per year**. $10,000 left for a year at 0.38% becomes $10,038, but you'd need about $10,374 to buy the same things. That's roughly **$9,676 in September-2025 dollars**, a loss of about $324 in purchasing power.

3. **$100 from 2020 buys about $77 worth today.** CPI-U (NSA) index: 260.280 in Sep 2020 vs. 336.940 in Sep 2026, so prices are up **≈29.5%** and $100 from Sep 2020 has the buying power of **≈$77.25** in Sep 2026. Over 10 years (Sep 2016 index 241.428): prices are up ≈39.6%, so $100 → **≈$71.65**.
   Source (index values): https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCNS (BLS CPI-U data via St. Louis Fed). Cross-check tool: https://www.bls.gov/data/inflation_calculator.htm (note: that calculator cannot use October 2025 as an endpoint).

4. **High-yield savings rates are about 10× the national average, and only a little above inflation.** Bankrate's November 2026 list shows top APYs of about **4.0%–4.35%** (as of Nov 6–7, 2026). Some require minimum balances, e.g. $5,000 for the 4.35% top rate.
   Source: https://www.bankrate.com/banking/savings/10-best-savings-account-rates/
   → Derived: at 4.25% APY vs. 3.7% inflation, the real return is only about **+0.5%**. Use this to explain the concept, not as a product recommendation. Don't name banks in the video.

5. **The Fed targets 2% inflation, and current rates are 3.75%–4.00%.** The Fed's longer-run goal is "inflation of 2 percent," measured by the PCE price index.
   Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/economy_14400.htm
   On **Oct 28, 2026**, the FOMC voted 10–2 to "maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 3-3/4 to 4 percent," after a 25 bp **hike on Sep 17, 2026**.
   Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm
   → Useful for one scene: the Fed raises rates partly to slow inflation. That eventually nudges savings yields up, but the national average lags far behind.

6. **Rule of 72 (math, not data):** 72 ÷ 3.7 ≈ **19.5 years** for prices to double at September 2026's pace. At the Fed's 2% target it would take about 36 years. This makes a good closing visual.

### Notes for the scriptwriter
- State dates on screen ("as of September 2026") because CPI updates monthly. The next CPI release could change the 3.7% figure before publish; recheck the BLS page right before rendering.
- Explain why we're using September and not October (the government shutdown data gap) in one neutral sentence, or skip it entirely.
- Keep it nonpartisan: no politicians and no tariff or blame narratives.
- No crypto, no gold, no named stocks or funds. Close with general concepts like "compare your rate with inflation," not "buy X."
