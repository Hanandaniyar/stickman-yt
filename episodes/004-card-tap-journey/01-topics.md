## 1. What Happens in the 2 Seconds After You Tap Your Card
*(52 chars)*. Alternatives: "Who Really Gets Paid When You Tap Your Card?" (45) / "The Hidden Fee Inside Every Cup of Coffee" (41)

**Angle.** A journey video. A stickman taps a card for a $5 coffee, time freezes, and we follow a tiny glowing "message" as it leaves the café and travels through real places: the **card terminal** (chip and NFC, encryption), the **payment processor's data center**, the **card network's hub** (routing), the **issuing bank** (fraud check, available credit), then "APPROVED" races back to the counter. Each stop is its own background, so the scenery tells the story. Part two, the money: that night, when the café closes, **settlement** happens and the café receives a little less than $5. We slice the coffee into pieces for the issuing bank (interchange), the network, and the processor. **The twist:** merchants build that cost into everyday prices, so even cash payers help fund card rewards. Mechanism only: no card brands recommended, and **no position** on pending swipe-fee legislation or the debit-cap court case.

**Main backgrounds (6):** café counter, card terminal close-up (inside view), processor data center, card network hub, issuing bank, café after closing (settlement and deposit).

**Why US viewers care.** Almost every purchase runs through this system. It explains rewards cards, "cash discount" signs, restaurant surcharges, and card minimums at small shops. Holiday shopping season starts now.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 5 | Credit cards, payments, banking, and fintech are top-tier advertisers. |
| Search interest | 4 | "how do credit card payments work," "credit card processing fees," and "how do credit card rewards work" are steady. Seasonal boost from holiday shopping. |
| Stickman fit / backgrounds | 5 | A literal trip through 6 distinct places, then back. Freeze-frame, a glowing message, a coffee sliced into pieces, 900 million taps a day as a flood of tiny cards. |
| Ad safety | 4 | Safe if we stay mechanical. Swipe-fee politics (legislation, lawsuits) must be avoided. |

**Key facts**
1. Nilson Report: US merchants paid **$198.25 billion** in card processing fees in **2025** (2024: $187.20 billion, up 8.7%), a weighted average of **1.59%** of purchase volume, on **$12.498 trillion** of card purchase volume (Issue 1303, March 2026).
   Sources: https://nilsonreport.com/merchant-processing-fees-industry-investment/ · https://nilsonreport.com/articles/merchant-processing-fees-in-the-united-states-2025/
2. Visa Fiscal 2025 Annual Report: **257.5 billion** transactions processed on Visa's network, and about **329 billion** Visa-branded transactions overall, roughly **901 million per day**.
   Source: https://s29.q4cdn.com/385744025/files/doc_downloads/2025/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf
3. Atlanta Fed payment diary (via Pew, **Sep 30, 2026**): cash fell from **33% of payments (2015) to 14% (2025)**, while credit cards rose from 18% to **34%**, the most-used method.
   Source: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/30/from-cash-to-credit-how-americans-payment-methods-have-changed-in-the-past-decade/
4. Federal Reserve Regulation II: the Fed publishes average debit interchange fees by network. An **Oct 2026 appeals court ruling** struck down the Fed's debit fee cap, so the cap is in legal limbo. **Don't script it as settled.** Mention debit vs. credit only in general terms.
   Sources: https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/regii-average-interchange-fee.htm · https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/10/federal-appeals-court-strikes-down-the-feds-debit-card-fee-cap/
5. Derived (our arithmetic): at the 1.59% average, a $5 coffee carries about **8¢** in processing cost. $198.25B ÷ $12.498T ≈ 1.59%, consistent with Nilson.

## 2. Why Your Paycheck Shrinks Before It Reaches You
*(48 chars)*. Alternatives: "Where Your Paycheck Goes Before You See It" (42) / "Salary vs. Take-Home Pay: Following Every Dollar" (48)

**Angle.** A stack of coins leaves the office as "gross pay" and walks to the employee's bank account. Along the way it makes stops, and each stop takes a slice: the **payroll office** (the calculator), the **401(k) vault** (pre-tax savings), the **health insurer** (premium share), the **Social Security office** (6.2%) and **Medicare at the hospital** (1.45%), and the **IRS building** (federal withholding). Then the much smaller stack arrives at the kitchen table, where a stickman reads a pay stub. The key ideas: which deductions lower taxable income and which don't, why Social Security tax **stops** above $192,300 in 2027 (a visible "gate" closing), why the employer secretly pays a matching 7.65% the worker never sees, and why withholding is an *estimate* that leads to a refund or a bill in April. Physics/tech flavor: payroll is an algorithm running the same steps every pay period. No tax-avoidance tips, no product names, state taxes mentioned only as "varies by state."

**Main backgrounds (7):** office floor, payroll department, 401(k) vault, hospital/insurer front desk, Social Security office, IRS building, kitchen table at home.

**Why US viewers care.** Every worker gets a pay stub and most never decode it. It ties together raises, health insurance costs, retirement saving, and tax refunds. January (first paycheck of 2027, new SS wage base, new withholding) is the perfect release window, and it complements episode 002 (tax brackets) without repeating it.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 5 | Taxes, payroll software, retirement, and health insurance are among the highest-paying categories. |
| Search interest | 5 | "paycheck calculator," "why is my paycheck so small," "what is FICA," and "how to read a pay stub" are evergreen, with a spike in January. |
| Stickman fit / backgrounds | 5 | One coin stack, 6 stops, a slice taken at each. The SS "gate" closing at $192,300 and the hidden employer match are strong visual reveals. |
| Ad safety | 5 | Purely educational and nonpartisan. |

**Key facts**
1. SSA, **Oct 14, 2026**: the Social Security taxable maximum rises to **$192,300 in 2027** (from $184,500 in 2026). The rate stays **6.2%** each for employee and employer, so the maximum per person is **$11,922.60**. Medicare is **1.45%** each with **no wage cap**, plus an extra **0.9%** employee Medicare tax above $200,000 (single). The 2027 COLA is **3.6%**.
   Sources: https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2026/oct/social-security-wage-base-and-cola-announced-for-2027/ · https://www.ssa.gov/news/press/factsheets/colafacts2027.pdf (blocked automated fetch; verify manually)
2. KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey, **Oct 22, 2026**: workers pay on average **$7,471/year** toward family coverage (up 9%, or $621, from 2025). The total family premium is **$28,303** (up 5%), so the employer pays the rest. Single coverage worker share is about **$1,672/year** (**to verify** in the full report; it comes from secondary coverage).
   Sources: https://www.kff.org/other-health/workers-now-pay-7471-on-average-annually-for-family-health-coverage-up-9-or-621-from-2025-benchmark-employer-health-benefits-survey-finds/ · https://www.kff.org/health-costs/report/2026-employer-health-benefits-survey/
3. IRS tax year 2027 (Nov 5, 2026): standard deduction **$16,600** single / **$33,200** married filing jointly. 2026 for comparison: **$16,100** single.
   Sources: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2027-taxpayers-continue-to-benefit-from-the-working-families-tax-cuts · https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill
4. 401(k) limits: 2026 deferral limit is **$24,500** (IRS, Nov 13, 2025). The **2027 limit is not official yet** as of Nov 14, 2026. Milliman's final forecast is **$25,500**. Refresh before scripting.
   Sources: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500 · https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/2027-irs-limits-forecast-final-estimates
5. Derived (illustrative, our arithmetic, 2026 single filer, no state tax, annualized then divided by 26 paychecks): $60,000 salary = **$2,308** gross per biweekly check. 6% to a 401(k) ≈ **$138**. Single health premium share ≈ **$64**. FICA (7.65% on wages after the pre-tax health premium) ≈ **$172**. Federal income tax (taxable ≈ $38,628 after 401(k), premium, and $16,100 standard deduction) ≈ **$169**. Take-home ≈ **$1,765**, about **76%** of gross. Real withholding tables differ slightly; present as "roughly."

## 3. Where Your Grocery Dollar Goes: From Farm to Shelf
*(50 chars)*. Alternatives: "Why Farmers Get Only 12 Cents of Your Food Dollar" (49) / "The Journey of a Thanksgiving Dinner, Dollar by Dollar" (54)

**Angle.** A road movie. A stickman pays $1 for food at the supermarket, and we rewind the dollar backwards through every place that touched the food: the **supermarket** (rent, staff, refrigerators), the **distribution warehouse** (cold storage, forklifts), the **truck on the highway** (diesel, driver), the **processing plant** (washing, cooking, packaging), and finally the **farm**, which ends up with only about **12 cents**. The surprise is that most of the price isn't the food. It's everything that happens *to* the food. Science flavor: cold-chain physics (why refrigeration is a big hidden cost), and why a restaurant meal has an even smaller farm share (the restaurant kitchen is one more expensive stop). Neutral: no blaming grocers, farmers, or politicians, no tariff talk.

**Main backgrounds (6):** supermarket aisle and checkout, distribution warehouse, highway with a refrigerated truck, food processing plant, farm/ranch, restaurant kitchen.

**Why US viewers care.** Groceries are a weekly expense everyone feels. The Thanksgiving week (Nov 26, 2026) and holiday meals make "why is food so expensive" a top question. It also explains why falling crop prices don't show up quickly at the checkout.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 3 | Food and grocery content earns less than pure finance. It can lean on budgeting and household finance ads, but it's the weakest CPM in this list. |
| Search interest | 4 | "why are groceries so expensive" is a strong evergreen query that peaks around holidays and CPI releases. |
| Stickman fit / backgrounds | 5 | The most varied and attractive backgrounds of the five, in a clear left-to-right journey. A dollar bill shrinking at each stop is self-explanatory. |
| Ad safety | 4 | Food prices are a political talking point right now. Stay with USDA/BLS mechanics, no blame. |

**Key facts**
1. USDA ERS Food Dollar Series (updated **Mar 10, 2026**; chart of note **Apr 28, 2026**): in **2024**, farms received **11.8 cents** of each dollar spent on domestically produced food (12.1 cents in 2023). The **marketing share was 88.2 cents**, covering transporting, processing, storing, wholesaling, and retailing. A new model was used in 2026, so don't compare with older releases.
   Sources: https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/charts-of-note/114074 · https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-dollar/summary-findings
2. Same ERS findings: **food services** (restaurants) took **38.6 cents** per food dollar in 2024, up 1.1 cents from 2023. Farmers and ranchers' own value added was roughly crops **2.5¢** + livestock **3.3¢**. A rising share of eating out is one reason the farm share keeps falling.
   Source: https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-dollar/summary-findings
3. Food-at-home vs. eating out: some coverage reports the farm share at **18.5¢** for groceries and **7.1¢** for restaurant food in 2024. **To verify** on the ERS data page before scripting.
   Source: https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-dollar-series
4. BLS CPI, September 2026 (released **Oct 14, 2026**): food at home rose **1.8%** over 12 months, food away from home rose **3.5%**, and all items rose **3.7%**.
   Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm
5. BLS CPI, October 2026 (released **Nov 10, 2026**): food at home rose **0.4%** in the month (beef +0.9%, snacks +1.0%). No 12-month change was published for October because October 2025 data were not collected during the 2025 shutdown. The next CPI is **Dec 10, 2026**.
   Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_11102026.htm

## 4. Is AI Making Your Electric Bill Higher? The Physics of Power
*(61 chars)*. Alternatives: "Why AI Needs So Much Electricity (and Who Pays)" (46) / "The Hidden Cost of Every AI Prompt" (34)

**Angle.** Follow one AI prompt from a phone in a kitchen to a **data center** (racks of hot chips, giant cooling fans), then follow the electricity backwards: **power plant**, **transmission towers**, the **grid control room**, and the bill landing on the kitchen table. Physics: chips turn almost all their electricity into heat, so cooling is a second big load. Money: the grid is a shared highway whose **capacity** costs money, and when demand outruns capacity, prices for that capacity rise and are spread across customers. Present both sides (natural gas prices, aging grid upgrades, and rate design matter too).

**Main backgrounds (5):** home kitchen with phone and electric bill, data center hall, power plant, transmission line corridor, grid control room.

**Why US viewers care.** Electricity is rising faster than overall inflation in many regions, and AI is part of daily life.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 4 | Tech plus energy/home is solid, a bit below pure personal finance. |
| Search interest | 4 | Trending, but regional (PJM states, Texas, Virginia). |
| Stickman fit / backgrounds | 4 | Strong, distinct locations. The mystery is weaker because the answer is "partly, depends where you live." |
| Ad safety | 4 | Contested and sometimes politicized locally. Stay evidence-based and neutral. |

**Key facts**
1. BLS CPI, September 2026 (**Oct 14, 2026**): electricity rose **4.4%** over 12 months. October 2026 (**Nov 10, 2026**): +0.9% in the month, no 12-month figure published (2025 shutdown gap).
   Sources: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm · https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_11102026.htm
2. EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook, **Nov 10, 2026**: average residential price about **18.2¢/kWh in 2026** (about 17.2¢ in 2025) and about **18.7¢ in 2027**. Total US consumption forecast at record highs in 2026 and 2027. Whether the November text explicitly names data centers differs between write-ups, so **check the EIA PDF directly**.
   Sources: https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/archives/nov26.pdf · https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/
3. DOE/LBNL (**Dec 20, 2024**): data centers used about **4.4%** of US electricity (176 TWh) in 2023, projected at **6.7%–12%** by 2028.
   Source: https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers
4. PJM 2028/29 capacity auction (**Jul 14, 2026**): cleared at the **$325/MW-day cap**, about **6.8 GW short** of the reliability target. PJM cited data-center-driven demand growth.
   Source: https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-capacity-auction-price-cap-reserve-shortfall-grows/825282/

## 5. Why Your Home Insurance Bill Keeps Going Up
*(46 chars)*. Alternatives: "I Never Filed a Claim. Why Did My Home Insurance Go Up?" (55) / "The Science Behind Your Rising Home Insurance" (45)

**Angle.** The mystery: a stickman's house has never had a claim, yet the premium jumps. We walk through places: (1) the **insurance office**, where everyone's premiums fill one shared tank; (2) a **street during a hailstorm** in another town, where the tank drains; (3) the **hardware store/lumber yard**, where rebuilding the same house costs more each year; (4) the **reinsurer's office**, because insurers buy insurance too; (5) back to the **kitchen table**, where the mortgage escrow payment rises. Physics: hail energy, wind load on roofs, and why insurers price by ZIP code. Present climate-loss data as insurer and regulator data, with no blame and no policy opinions.

**Main backgrounds (6):** suburban street with the house, insurance office (shared tank), stormy street in another town, hardware store/lumber yard, reinsurer's office, kitchen table with escrow statement.

**Why US viewers care.** Lenders require homeowners insurance, so a higher premium raises the monthly housing payment. It's a major affordability issue in Florida, Texas, California, and the Gulf states, and increasingly elsewhere.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CPM | 5 | Insurance is one of the most expensive ad categories on YouTube. |
| Search interest | 4 | "why is my home insurance going up" spikes at renewal and after disasters. Strongest regionally. |
| Stickman fit / backgrounds | 4 | Good, varied places. A bit more explanation-heavy than #1–3. |
| Ad safety | 3 | Climate framing can read as political, and disasters are sensitive. Careful, neutral wording required. |

**Key facts**
1. NAIC national homeowners market analysis (reported **Aug 5, 2026**, data 2018–2024): average premium per policy rose in **every NAIC region**, by **18.3% to 43.3% after inflation** (about 2.4%–5.3% a year). Insurer-initiated non-renewals rose **96%–216%** depending on region.
   Source: https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/naic-releases-first-of-its-kind-national-analysis-of-homeowners-insurance-market-trends
2. US Treasury Federal Insurance Office (**Jan 16, 2025**, data 2018–2022): premiums rose **8.7% faster than inflation**. Policyholders in the **riskiest 20% of ZIP codes** paid **$2,321** on average, **82% more** than in the lowest-risk 20%.
   Source: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2791
3. NerdWallet 2026 report (commercial data): the typical premium reached **$2,564** in early 2026, up **24%** from Q1 2023 to Q1 2026. Matic (mid-2026): renewal increases slowed to **10.6%** in H1 2026 vs. 19.4% in 2025. Commercial estimates differ by sample, so show a range and label them.
   Sources: https://www.nerdwallet.com/insurance/homeowners/home-insurance-pricing-report-2026 · https://matic.com/blog/2026-home-insurance-report/
4. Insurify (2026): national average about **$3,012** in H1 2026. Florida remains the most expensive state (about **$8,471**). National averages vary by source ($2,400–$3,100).
   Sources: https://insurify.com/homeowners-insurance/insights/rates-increase-first-half-2026 · https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/property/floridians-pay-181-more-for-home-insurance-than-us-average--571385.aspx
