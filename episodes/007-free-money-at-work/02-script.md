# 02 — Script: Shorts "Your Job Is Handing Out Free Money"

- **Format:** YouTube Shorts, vertical 1080×1920, target 45–50 s. Same style as ep005/ep006.
- **Word count:** 116 words (≈ 48 s with pauses and tail).
- **Promise kept:** "free money" = the employer 401(k) match. "Hiding" is flipped on screen: the boss isn't hiding it, it's in the boring fine print.
- **Facts (01-research.md):** about 1 in 5 participants don't save enough to get the full match (Fidelity Q2 2026: 81% do) [F1]. $60,000 salary, 100% match up to 4% → $2,400/yr; saving 2% → $1,200/yr missed [own calc]. $1,200/yr for 30 years at 7%/yr after inflation → $113,353 [own calc; assumption, not guaranteed]. Vesting: employer money can be forfeited if you leave early; max 3-yr cliff or 6-yr graded (IRS) [I1]. Old 2015 "$24 billion" figure deliberately not used.

## Narration (one line = one scene)

1. Your job is handing out free money. One in five people never grab it.
2. It's the 401(k) match.
3. You save part of your paycheck. Your company adds money on top.
4. Earn sixty thousand, with a match up to four percent?
5. Save four percent, and they add twenty-four hundred dollars a year.
6. Save just two percent? You get half.
7. Twelve hundred dollars a year, left on the table.
8. Over thirty years, at seven percent after inflation, not guaranteed,
9. that's over a hundred thousand dollars. Gone.
10. One catch: some company money only becomes yours after a few years. Vesting.
11. Your boss isn't hiding it. It's just in the boring fine print.
12. And boring can be worth six figures.

- 9: the bag grows into a "$113,000" tower… then poof, "GONE" (dust cloud).

- 11: boss stickman shrugs; a giant contract unrolls with tiny print, a magnifying glass finds "MATCH"; stickman yawns.
- 12: a yawning, boring document puts on sunglasses and stands on a pile of money: "$100,000+"; channel name.
